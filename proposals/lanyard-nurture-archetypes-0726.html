Lanyard Health, Nurture Sequence Framework

Four kinds of buyer. Four different jobs for the follow up.

Drawn from real calls. Each section moves from who they are, to the email sent the same day as the call, to the sequence that follows if a live next call couldn't get locked in. Tap "Full Profile" on any archetype for the underlying detail.

A nurture sequence starts where a live call ends. The best outcome from any call is a calendar invite sent before hanging up, the next conversation locked in while the energy is still there. Nurture is the fallback, not the plan.

Five ways to help someone understand what's going on

Five independent categories to pull from, not five required steps. A sequence might lean on one or two of these the whole way through, or mix all five. The order below is just for reference.

If the call doesn't land, the sequence that follows
  • 02SolutionWhere the friction usually shows up in deals like yours▸
    To Matt, from Kay

    Following up on delegated credentialing. In similar staffing and leasing arrangements, the friction usually shows up in one of two places: either the payer has never delegated to a staffing model before and needs to be walked through how your structure works, or your existing verification process needs to be documented more formally before they'll even consider it.

    Worth knowing going in, so neither becomes a surprise once you're in the room with a payer.

  • 03SolutionWhat we've seen work, and not work, for similar structures▸
    To Matt, from Kay

    A few patterns worth sharing from similar conversations. What tends to work: coming in with a clear, documented verification process and a specific ask, rather than an open ended request for delegation. What tends to stall things: asking a payer to delegate credentialing for a specialty they've never delegated before without another comparable group as precedent.

    Happy to talk through where Sylvan's model fits into that, if it's useful.

  • 04SymptomA pattern worth knowing before you're deep into negotiations▸
    To Matt, from Kay

    One thing worth flagging before you get too far into payer conversations. Practices that go into delegation discussions without a clear roster process in place often end up restarting the conversation later, once the payer asks for something they can't produce yet.

    Might be worth having that roster structure ready before your first real conversation with a payer, even if delegation is still a few months out.

  • 05SolutionWhat typically triggers a payer to reconsider▸
    To Matt, from Kay

    One more thing that might be useful heading into your payer conversations. Delegation requests tend to move faster when there's already a comparable group of similar size or specialty that the payer has delegated to before. If Sylvan has any visibility into which of your target payers have granted delegation to a staffing or leasing model elsewhere, that's worth leading with.

    Let me know if any of the payer conversations have moved. Happy to jump on one if it would help.

If pricing isn't locked yet

Not every prospect gets an exact quote in the immediate follow up, and that's fine. Pricing doesn't have to be finalized to send this email.

What does have to exist instead: a clear list of the questions that determine contract value. Things like provider count, payer mix, states, and whatever else changes what it actually costs to service a given practice. Once that list exists, quoting becomes fast and confident instead of a guess. This is this week's homework.

  • 04ProofThe math other practices your size are running▸
    To Piyush, from Kay

    Sharing something that might be useful for your comparison. Practices with four or so locations and 15 to 20 providers, similar to Sevi Health, typically see somewhere between ten and fifteen thousand dollars in delayed or lost revenue per provider when credentialing gaps go unnoticed. Most of that comes from applications sitting untouched, not from the credentialing process itself taking too long.

    Just a data point worth having on hand.

  • 05SymptomA pattern worth knowing before it becomes urgent▸
    To Piyush, from Kay

    No news to report, just a pattern worth having in your back pocket. Practices with one dedicated in house credentialing person tend to be fine day to day, right up until a hiring wave or a new location hits. That's usually when things start slipping, and by the time it's visible, providers are already sitting unbillable for weeks.

    Not saying that's where Sevi Health is headed, just worth knowing before it becomes relevant.
