The Frame

You don't need a strategy. You need a sentence.

Every automation worth building starts as one of these, said out loud by someone who was tired:

"It would be great if we didn't have to do this."
"I spend too much time on this instead of the actual work."
"We're about to hire for this, and we shouldn't have to."

Sharpening 01 — Talk, Don't Type

Stop typing. Start talking.

Turn on dictation and ramble. Say the messy version — the one with the background, the frustration, the thing you tried last year that didn't work.

Your spoken version carries three times the context of your tidy typed version. Context is what makes the output good. Typing makes you compress, and compression is exactly what you don't want here.
Windows — Win + H
Mac — press Fn twice
Phone — the mic on your keyboard
Sharpening 02 — Delete a Phrase

Delete "prompt engineering" from your vocabulary

It's a conversation, not an incantation. There is no magic wording you're failing to guess.

You would never engineer a prompt to a colleague. You'd tell them what you need, they'd ask a question, you'd clarify, and you'd go back and forth until it was right. Do that. If the first answer is wrong, say what's wrong with it.
"That's too formal"
"You missed the part about the grant"
"Shorter, and less like a brochure"
Sharpening 03 — Context Is the Whole Game

Feed it your actual documents

Most bad output is a context problem wearing a prompt costume. The model isn't guessing badly. It's guessing without your last grant report, your board deck, your program description.

Upload the real thing. Every time.

Sharpening 04 — Partner, Not Replacement

Thought partner, not thought replacement

Argue with it. Ask it what's wrong with your plan. Ask what a skeptical funder would push back on. Ask what you're not seeing.

If it agrees with everything you say, you're using it wrong. Agreement is not help. You already had your opinion before you opened the tab.
"What's the weakest part of this?"
"Argue the other side"
"What would a funder ask that I can't answer?"
Sharpening 05 — You Can Build Now

The tool your organization needs may not exist as software you can buy. It used to be that the answer was a developer you couldn't afford, so you used a spreadsheet instead.

That's over. The barrier now is knowing clearly what you want. Which is why we spent the first ten minutes on the sentence.

Sharpening 06 — Deep, Not Wide

Go deep with your tools, not wide

There is a new AI tool every week promising to do something magical for your organization. Almost none of them will matter to you. Chasing them is how you end up with eleven subscriptions and no capacity.

This field is also moving fast enough that sinking months into one big build is a bad bet — whatever you built against will have moved by the time you finish. So keep the stack lean, and get good at what's in it.

I specialize in two things: Claude and Google Workspace. Not because nothing else is good, but because Workspace is far more powerful than most people who use it daily realize. The automation available inside that one ecosystem — before you connect a single outside tool — covers most of what a nonprofit actually needs.

Be more excited about deepening what you already have than about finding what's next.

Sharpening 07 — Human in the Loop

You are the editor. Permanently.

This is not a phase you graduate from. Nothing leaves your organization without a person reading it. Not because the tool is bad, but because your name is on it.

And while we're here: every one of these tools has a training setting that decides whether your conversations improve the model. Go find yours today. If you are putting participant data anywhere near a chatbot, a personal account is not the right container — you need a business account with an actual agreement behind it.

Under the Hood

These are not six projects

Every failure in every aisle traces to the same root. The data lived in someone's inbox, a spreadsheet nobody owns, and one person's head.
The substrate

Information capture

One place where what happened gets recorded, the moment it happens.

Six views

Same data, different questions

The funder asks one thing. The board asks another. Your newsletter asks a third. Same underlying record.

The real goal

Ten seconds

You can answer any question about your own work in ten seconds. That's the whole vision.

What Happens After You Leave

It's going to feel busier before it feels lighter

Add a lane to a highway and you get more traffic, not less. Same thing here. Clearing a bottleneck reveals work that was always there, queued up behind it.

Month one often feels busier. That is the system working, not failing. It settles.

And then something stranger happens: these become invisible. Within a few weeks you won't remember what it was like without them, which means nobody will ever thank you for building them. Build them anyway.
