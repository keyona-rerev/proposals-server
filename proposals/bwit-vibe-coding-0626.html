You do not have to be a coder

Think about language literacy. It is not just knowing what the words mean. It is knowing what they mean to other people, and how the pieces fit together. Code literacy works the same way, and it is dimensional. The level you have is what decides how far you can go.

"Hello, World," you are in.

02 · The building blocks

Four pieces run almost
everything you touch.

Front end

HTML, CSS, JS

The layer that runs in your browser. HTML structures the page, CSS styles it, and JavaScript makes it do things. Everything you see rendered on a website is built on these three.

Back end

Python

Behind the front end sits the server side: the logic and the data. That is where a general purpose language like Python usually lives. Different layer, different job.

Why it matters

The model's home turf

Because these are everywhere on the web and on GitHub, they are exactly what LLMs have the most training data on. That is why the models are so strong here. For most of what you build, this is all you need.

▼
Calibration prompt, capped at the read-back
I want you to walk me through building an "Offerings x Journeys" grid for my business. It is a calibration exercise: we sort every offer I have into three revenue tiers, then map how each kind of customer actually travels through them. How I want you to run this, and this part matters most. Do not interview me with a blank list of questions. Assume you already know a fair amount about my business from what I have shared with you. At every stage, lead with YOUR best interpretation first, your read of my offers, my buyers, my pricing, my real path to revenue, and then ask me to correct it. I will either confirm it or say "actually, no, it is like this." Reacting to a draft is far easier for me than starting from nothing, so always propose first and never hand me an empty prompt. If you genuinely do not have enough context about me yet, ask me once for a short overview or a document, then switch straight back into this propose-and-correct mode. Work through these stages one at a time. After each one, stop and get my reaction before moving on. 1) THE MODEL. Explain the three tiers in plain terms. Attraction (free to about $99: builds trust and earns the first yes, does not pay bills), Core (about $50 to $500: productized and repeatable, the easy middle door most people are missing), and High-ticket (about $1,500 to $5,000 and up: done-for-you, one to one, priced as real work, this is what pays the bills). Then check one thing before we go further: does this lens even fit my business? It fits if I sell expertise or services directly. It does NOT fit a fund or enterprise infrastructure sold as one large contract. If I am one of those, say so, and we either stop or reframe around entry point versus full engagement instead. 2) MY OFFERS, SORTED. Present your best guess at every offer I have, sorted into the three tiers. Make the high-ticket bucket explicit, name each distinct offering inside it. I will correct anything misfiled or missing. 3) MY BUYERS. Propose the distinct customer types I sell to. For each one, mark it Live (paying me today) or Target (I want them, but have not proven it). Push me to be honest here. 4) HOW THEY ENTER. For each buyer, tell me how you think they actually arrive: warm, through my network or a referral, or cold, through a funnel I have built. Then let me correct it. 5) THE JOURNEY, HONESTLY. For each buyer, map their path down the three tiers, what attracts them, what they buy first, where they land. Where a buyer skipped a rung, or where I have no built path, SHOW THAT AS A GAP. Do not invent a clean funnel. An empty cell that is true is worth more than a full one that is fiction. 6) THE STRUCTURAL LAYERS. Tag each high-ticket offer by its shape: project (one-time), retainer (recurring), or cohort (per-program). Keep the entry marker (warm or cold) visible on each buyer. 7) READ IT BACK TO ME. With the map filled in, tell me what it reveals: where does my revenue actually come from versus where have I built the funnel? Where is the gap between my proven buyers and my built paths? Name the single build that would matter most. Start with the model, and remember, propose first, then let me correct.

06 · How I actually work

Go deep, and keep
a second opinion open.

Depth over breadth

Go deep with few tools

I have shrunk my stack on purpose. Mostly Claude plus Google Workspace. Depth in a few tools beats scattering across many. How much you put in is your call: that word, discernment, is the whole point. I am not handling confidential work, so I am wide open. Yours may be different.

The two-tab method

One build, one consultant

Primary tab: a Claude chat where I keep a heavy hand on the build. Second tab: ChatGPT or DeepSeek as a second opinion. The same way you do not know everything, no single model does either. A second set of eyes catches what the first was sure about.

06 · When it breaks (and it will)

Do not trust the first story.

The trap

"Oh, I see the problem"

Several times a day a model will confidently announce the cause and dive in before it actually knows. Your move: pause it. "Who told you that? Where did you get this? Did you just make that up?"

The discipline

Go to the logs

Right click, Inspect, read the actual diagnostic information. You cannot diagnose without data. When it gets really sticky, ask the model to write a briefing for another conversation, paste that into the second tab, then bring the answer back: "here is what the other model said, evaluate what fits."

This is experimentation, and it is the whole advantage: you get to try and fail faster, more focused, and higher quality than ever. But only if you stay engaged. It runs on your attention, not the model's.

06 · The habit that compounds

End every session
with a retrospective.

At the end of a build, I have the system write down what worked, what broke, and what to do differently. It is how my systems get smarter, faster, and stop repeating the same mistakes. When you are truly in tune with your tools, you get higher quality and higher pace at once, with less tax on your mind. Hold that thought. It is where we land at the end.
