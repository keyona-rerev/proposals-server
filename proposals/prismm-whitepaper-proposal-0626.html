The Inheritance Liability Report

A paired research document built to travel from a sales conversation into a CFO's hands — and make the case without Martha in the room.

What this white paper is
An original research report combining FDIC and NCUA public data with a practitioner survey of community bank deposit and operations leaders.
Built to quantify the deposit liability sitting in deceased and dormant account populations — and expose the operational gap that keeps most institutions from seeing or managing it.
Designed as a conversion asset: something a champion can hand to a CFO or board after a conversation with Martha that makes the case in the language executives already trust.
Champion thesis
Thesis
We have a measurable deposit liability sitting in our deceased and dormant account population that is not showing up anywhere in our risk framework — and institutions our size are losing an estimated X per billion in assets because of it. We are vulnerable without a tech-driven inheritance infrastructure.

Part 01 — Public record
The Liability in the Data
We use trusted, publicly available FDIC and NCUA data to prove the risk already exists at scale inside institutions like yours.
FDIC call report analysis showing dormant and deceased account concentration across sub-$10B institutions — translated into estimated exposure per billion in assets.
NCUA and FDIC enforcement patterns showing where this liability surfaces as a named regulatory risk.
Curinos $11M per $1B applied to concentration data to produce a liability range a CFO can act on.
Part 02 — Original research
The Operational Gap
We use original survey research to show that most institutions have no infrastructure to manage the risk Part 01 proves.
Visibility: Do practitioners track beneficiary retention or know their deceased account volume at any given time?
Ownership: Who owns a customer death inside the institution — and how often is the answer nobody clearly?
Infrastructure: What would have to exist for this to be managed differently — and why doesn't it today?
Why we conceived it this way
1
Conversion asset, not thought leadership.
The primary question was not what the buyer would find interesting in the abstract. It was what job this document is doing at a specific moment in the sales conversation. The answer was Step 3: a champion leaving Martha's meeting and needing to carry the case upstairs without her. That determined everything else about the structure.
2
Public data leads because the CFO already trusts it.
The CFO receiving this document has not met Martha. They have no reason to trust a fintech vendor's framing of the problem. FDIC and NCUA data is data they already trust and can locate their institution inside. Leading with that source removes the credibility question before it gets asked.
3
The survey exposes a risk gap, not a demand signal.
A survey that asks whether bankers want a better wealth transfer solution is a marketing survey. The survey we designed asks whether they have visibility, ownership, and infrastructure around the death event. That produces a finding the CFO reads as a risk assessment — not a pitch.
Part 01 is external proof. Part 02 is the internal mirror. Together they give the champion something to hand upstairs that speaks the CFO's language without Martha in the room.
