Job Lifecycle9 steps · one open at a time
01
CAPTURE
INTAKE
›
A calendar event tagged [Agent], or an email forwarded to the agent address, becomes a queued row on the board. Mail from anyone outside the allowlist is turned away and reported. Nothing else can start a job.
02
CLAIM
WORKER
›
One row per run. The worker writes a random token, waits, then re-reads it. If the token changed, another run took the job and this one stops. A spreadsheet has no atomic write — the re-read is what breaks the tie.
03
DEFINE
WORKER
›
Before any work happens, the job gets an end goal in one sentence and a list of deliverables — the concrete artifacts that must exist when it is finished. A job with no defined end can never be closed, so it would chase forever.
04
SPLIT
WORKER
›
The work divides three ways: what can be done now, what can be drafted, and what genuinely needs a human decision. The first two get done. The third is parked as a short numbered list — never more than three questions.
05
SEND
APPS SCRIPT
›
One email, one job, on its own thread — never a digest of everything in flight. It fits on a phone screen: the context, the end goal, the deliverables proposed for approval, and the decisions needed. Anything longer hands the work back while appearing to have finished it.
06
CHASE
FOLLOW-UP
›
A parked job is chased after two days, then every two days, as a reply on its own thread. The wording escalates rather than repeating. After four attempts the job goes quiet and marks itself stalled. Weekdays only, three chases a day, oldest first.
07
ANSWER
HUMAN
›
Four one-word replies cost nothing: close ends it, snooze 5d pauses it, waiting on [name] marks someone else as the blocker and slows the chase, confirm [id] settles a deliverable. Anything else is treated as a real answer and re-queues the job.
08
VERIFY
FOLLOW-UP
›
Every check looks for the artifact, never for its absence. A sent message on the thread. A file created after the job started. A doc modified since. A draft sitting in Drafts is not a sent email — and a deleted draft is not delivery either. Anything a machine cannot check honestly is marked manual and waits for a human.
09
CLOSE
OUTPUT
›
The job closes when every deliverable is verified, confirmed, or waived — not when anything claims to have finished. A closing note lands on the same thread listing what shipped. Nothing is marked done on someone's word, and nothing open is silently dropped.
