The $900B TAM refers to total power generation units, a much broader frame than fuel cell analyst scope; the IC should weight the SAM and beachhead figures more heavily.

Independent Market Research: 10-Year Outlook

EV charging infrastructure is one of the most consistently high-growth markets in climate tech, with analysts converging on 20-25% annual growth through the early 2030s, and the multi-unit dwelling segment Kiwi targets is independently identified as a major share of future charge points.

Growth signal: Grand View Research projects the EV charging infrastructure market from $40.22B in 2025 to $238.82B by 2033 at a 25.0% CAGR [1]. Read against company claims: Kiwi's $112B-by-2030 TAM sits inside the independent projection band (analysts put the market between $113B and $239B by 2032-2033). The stronger independent support is segment-level: PwC's ~6 million multi-unit residential charge points by 2030 directly validates the beachhead where fixed-charger economics fail and Kiwi's robot-per-building model competes.

Independent Market Research: 10-Year Outlook

Mars sits at the intersection of two very different markets: a mature, slow-growing commodity chemical (acrylonitrile) and a fast-growing carbon-utilization overlay. The venture-scale story is the second one.

The commodity base is mature: Grand View Research sizes global acrylonitrile at $11.9B in 2024 growing to $15.0B by 2030 at just 4.0% CAGR [1]; IndustryARC sees $20B by 2030 at 4.4% [2]. Notably, Grand View identifies carbon fiber as the fastest-growing acrylonitrile application segment [1], which independently supports Mars's stated carbon fiber expansion market. The polyacrylamide beachhead grows from $5.5B (2022) to $9.1B by 2030 at 6.5%, with wastewater treatment the largest application [3].

The carbon overlay is where venture growth lives: CO₂ utilization is projected to grow from $4.57B to $11.15B by 2032 at a 13.6% CAGR [4]. Mordor Intelligence sizes carbon capture and utilization at $3.84B in 2026 with utilization services as the fastest-growing segment at 9.45% CAGR as companies commercialize CO₂-derived fuels and chemicals [5]. Under the broadest definitions, carbon capture utilization chemicals are projected from $58.5B in 2024 to $476.3B by 2034 at 20.5% CAGR, with chemicals and petrochemicals the largest end-use as buyers seek CO₂-derived intermediates for net-zero supply chains [6]. Chemical production is repeatedly cited as the fastest-growing utilization application.

Read against company claims: Mars's $11B polyacrylamide beachhead figure runs slightly ahead of Grand View's $9.1B 2030 estimate [3]; the IC should treat $9B to $11B as the credible band. The $40B-by-2050 acrylonitrile TAM is a reasonable extrapolation of a ~4% CAGR base. The key insight for evaluation: Mars's growth driver is not commodity acrylonitrile growth (low single digits) but the low-carbon premium layer, where independent forecasts run 13% to 20%+ annually, plus the drop-in advantage that avoids customer retooling.
