Corporate
EngagementStrategy

Two lanes, one boundary. Corporate money does not do one thing for a fund like ours. It does two unrelated things, for two different buyers, on two different timelines. Running them as one motion is why the pitch has been hard to write.

Source · 40-minute field interview with Niels Hagege, Head of Development & Partnerships, Cycle Momentum (Montreal). Cycle Momentum runs the Circle of Allies: 23 industrial members including Rio Tinto, ArcelorMittal, Saint-Gobain, Schneider Electric and Hydro-Québec, meeting quarterly, in person, closed door. Two and a half years of operating history behind every constraint on this page.

The finding that reorganizes everything

There is a line corporates will not cross

Industrials pay for pilots. They do not pay for the work that happens before a pilot is possible. Cycle Momentum spent real energy trying to pull corporates into projects earlier and called it wasted time.

Above the line

Discovery

Scouting, qualification, convening, education, ecosystem-building. Necessary, unglamorous, and someone else's bill.

  • LP capital
  • Foundations & philanthropy
  • Federal / provincial innovation budgets
  • Professional-services sponsors
Corporates will not fund above this line

Below the line

Deployment

Pilot execution against a named operational need, with a technology that has already been qualified by someone else.

  • Industrial corporates
  • $100K – $300K per pilot
  • TRL 6 and above only
  • Sourced by warm intro

BTC's structural position

Our discovery is already paid for. LPs fund diligence through the fund. We do not arrive asking a corporate to underwrite a search — we arrive with companies already qualified, already at TRL 6+, already holding paying customers. That is exactly the gap Niels described in the Canadian market, and it is a stronger opening position than an accelerator has.

The split

Two buyers, two motions

Money that comes to BTC comes from firms that sell into the ecosystem. Money that goes to portcos comes from firms that buy from it. Pitch either one at the other and neither converts.

Sponsor lane

Who pays
Law firms, banks, accounting firms, insurers, EMDs, wealth managers. Firms whose customer is the ecosystem.
What they want
Client acquisition and deal flow. Visibility in a room they cannot convene themselves.
What comes back
Firm revenue, near term. This is the 2026 answer to the revenue question.
Proof already in hand
CIBC confirmed at ~$3,500 for the New York dinner. Price the next two off that.

Pilot lane

Who pays
Industrial corporates, utilities, infrastructure owners. Firms with decarbonization targets and an operational problem.
What they want
Revenue transacted with the startup. Not returns. Vinci runs a $60M fund and does not optimize it for ROI.
What comes back
Portfolio value, not firm revenue. Portco revenue becomes up rounds and markups. Size this as a fund investment, not a revenue line.
Where the work sits
Close to the business unit that owns the problem. Not the CVC desk.

Co-investment lane

Who pays
Corporate venture arms who have watched a portco perform inside their own operation.
What they want
Exposure to a company they already trust operationally. The cheque follows the pilot, never leads it.
What comes back
Fund II and III. Co-investors first, LPs after. The real prize, and the slowest.
Dependency
Entirely downstream of the pilot lane working. Cannot be pursued directly.

From the field

Constraints to design around

Each of these came from someone with two and a half years of running the motion, plus six or seven before that in Europe. Treat them as findings, not opinions.

C-01

The KPI moved from returns to revenue

ArcelorMittal's head of investment spends 80% of his time with internal business-unit customers and 20% on deal flow. His single measure is how much revenue flows between the corporate and the startup. The investment is an add-on to an operational relationship, never the point of it.

C-02

TRL 6 is a hard floor

Below TRL 6 corporates say they are interested and then never engage. Everyone wastes time. Our portfolio clears this — by the time a company reaches us there is technical validation, market validation, and a customer who has paid real money.

C-03

Depth beats breadth, and it isn't close

You cannot understand forty corporations' problems deeply enough to be useful. Their answer was 23 members, quarterly, face to face, closed door, 85% attendance. Ours should be one corporate whose operational need aligns to one or two portcos.

C-04

Invite two people per organization

The senior, polished, outward-facing person often does not know what happens inside the company. The person who can actually decide is hands-on and does not want the spotlight. Invite both. Keep the first relationship, use the second one.

C-05

Large-format events do not close anything

Useful for surfacing names and nothing more. Every corporate engagement worth having requires customized understanding of that company's specific challenge. Budget conference time as sourcing, never as conversion.

C-06

Canadian industrials sit outside the innovation ecosystem

The large industrials in their circle are European or American, rarely Canadian, and are not connected to startups day to day. That disconnect is a wedge for a Canadian fund with Canadian portcos and documented pilot results.

Targeting

Warm doors before cold maps

The instinct is to build a corporate map. The better move is to work the doors that are already open and underused.

Tier 1 · Already validated us

Co-validators

  • GM Canada — the single warmest industrial door we have
  • Shell GameChanger
  • Breakthrough Energy
  • Government of Ontario

Tier 2 · Already bought

Portco customers

Every company in the portfolio has already sold a pilot to someone. Those buyers are corporates who have already said yes to climate tech, and their innovation and procurement people know their counterparts.

This list does not exist yet. Building it is the first real task.

Tier 3 · Adjacent

Network adjacencies

Investment committee relationships, LP employers and boards, Cycle Momentum's investor community under the signed MOU, and the ecosystem partners already carrying our name.

Open hypothesis — verify before committing

Utilities and infrastructure owners may touch more of the portfolio at once than chemicals or automotive would, which would make them the highest-leverage single sector rather than a one-company fit. That depends entirely on the actual buyer categories for Serenity Power and Resiliocs Intelligence. Confirm those before this becomes a plan.

The build

Pilot exhibition

The one concrete thing to execute. Not a showcase of companies — a showcase of results, with the people who paid for them in the room.

What it is

A closed session where portfolio companies present completed and in-flight pilots, with outcome numbers, integration timelines, and the paying customer present to speak to what actually happened. Niels's addition was the customer: it converts a showcase into third-party proof and it is the reason this sells where a program would not.

It sits entirely below the line. We are not asking anyone to fund discovery. We are showing qualified technology with evidence attached.

Dependency to test first

The hard constraint is not the corporates, it is the founders. Every founder has to be willing to ask a paying customer to vouch on the record. Some will say yes immediately. Some will not have a customer who can speak publicly. If only one portco can field a speakable customer, the format collapses. Test this before building anything around it.

Spec

  • Format Virtual. Suits our bandwidth and our geography.
  • Size Small and closed. Not a panel, not an audience.
  • Guests Two per corporate — the visible one and the operator.
  • Sourcing Warm introduction only. No open registration.
  • Content Outcome numbers, speed to integration, customer testimony.
  • Timing Late 2026 or early 2027, gated on founder readiness.

Consequences

What this changes

Two things in flight need repositioning based on the boundary finding.

Climate Tech Exit Lab

  • Was A program a corporate sponsor would fund.
  • Now Education sits above the line. Corporates are structurally the wrong funder. Its funders are foundations, government innovation budgets, and sponsor-lane firms.
  • And The investor-education half is the strongest non-dinner sponsor pitch we have. A room of high-net-worth investors learning how climate exits work is a direct client-acquisition channel for wealth managers, law firms and EMDs.
  • Honestly Its second function is converting HNW investors into people who understand the asset class well enough to write into Fund II. It is LP pipeline wearing a program's clothes. Good reason to build it — size it against Fund II, not 2026 revenue.

Intern scope

  • Was Map corporate sponsors.
  • Now Map the warm doors already inside the network: co-validators, portco customers, IC and LP adjacencies. Warm before cold.
  • Now Run the informational interview circuit. One 40-minute call produced six hard constraints. Twelve more is a body of knowledge nobody else in our position holds, and it is genuinely ownable work.
  • Now Build the sponsor-side prospect list separately, on different qualification criteria.
  • Now Keep a running findings file so constraints stop living only inside call transcripts.

Before this becomes a plan

Open questions

  • Which of the four portcos have completed a pilot with a customer who could speak publicly about it?
  • Does the near-term revenue target sit entirely on the sponsor lane, or are we still counting on corporate dollars inside 2026?
  • The tax-credit route — corporates directing credits toward ecosystem funding. Real information, or a hypothesis that needs research before it enters a plan?
  • Is NYC Climate Week the venue to run the interview circuit at volume, or is it a warm-intro venue for the pilot lane?
  • What are the actual buyer categories for Serenity Power and Resiliocs Intelligence?

Standing compliance note

As this moves toward US corporates, the same line applies as with the New York dinner. Corporate engagement conversations stay operational and do not drift into fund solicitation. The $10K minimum stays out of anything US-facing.
