01  /  The problem is not the software

It is that one account holds everything, and it is not hers

The complaint about all in one platforms is usually that they are bloated. That is not the real issue here. The real issue is that every function below lives inside a single login a third party controls, so there is no way to change one thing without asking permission for all of it.

One account, nine jobs

Contacts, sending, automation, forms, pages, link tracking, image hosting, suppression history, and sender reputation. All bundled. All rented. All held by someone else.

03  /  Decisions

Settled, with the reason attached

Each of these closed a question that was genuinely open. The reasoning is written down so none of it gets relitigated in three weeks.

One person, one record

Prospect and member are the same human at different stages. One table, one status field, no integration to maintain between two halves of the same list.

Sequences are built, not bought

No CRM sells the sequence feature without also selling nine things she does not need. Apps Script does the timing for nothing.

The monthly send is not a sequence

It is a broadcast on a calendar. Same content, same time, everyone. Only the welcome flow is an actual sequence, and it is two or three emails.

Deliverability stays rented

The one liability we refuse to take on. Self hosted sending is free in licensing and expensive in consequences.

Machine state and human labels stay apart

Step counters and send dates live in Supabase where nothing gets hand edited. Notes and labels live where Shella can change them without breaking a send.

The alias is the classification

The agent never guesses which block a forwarded note belongs to, because the address it was sent to already said.

Members and subscribers only

This system is not for prospects. Marketing and sales stay on separate rails, with separate lists and separate infrastructure.

Do not buy a CRM yet

Buying one now solves a problem she does not have and adds a bill she would carry for years. The trigger to buy is named below, not guessed at.

ARMED defaults to false

Every trigger checks it before acting. The system can be fully installed, fully tested, and still unable to email a single person until Shella changes one value.

04  /  The hidden inventory

What the bundle was quietly doing for her

These are the jobs nobody thinks about until the account is cancelled. Each one has to be handled before extraction, not after, and most of them depend on getting an export from the current vendor first.

What it holdsWhy it mattersIf it is not handled
Suppression and unsubscribe historyEvery person who unsubscribed, bounced, or filed a complaint over the life of the list.Re-mailing people who opted out. A compliance problem and the fastest way to destroy a brand new sending domain.
The unsubscribe link in delivered mailEvery email already sitting in someone's inbox routes its opt out through the vendor account.Those links stop resolving the day the account closes, which is live exposure on mail already sent.
Consent and opt in timestampsThe record of when and how each person agreed to be emailed.No provable consent trail for a list that has existed for years.
Image hosting for past emailsEvery image in every past newsletter is served from vendor infrastructure tied to their account.Every archived email breaks visually at once.
Link tracking redirectsEvery call to action in every past email points at a vendor redirect, not the real destination.Historic links go dead, including anything shared or bookmarked.
Sending domain reputationYears of warmup built on the current marketing subdomain.Reputation does not transfer. A new sending subdomain starts cold and needs warmup built into the timeline.

05  /  The boundary

Two systems that never share a wire

The most common way a small business quietly wrecks its email is by running cold outreach and member communication through the same infrastructure. That is a structural decision, not a settings toggle, so it gets made once here and enforced everywhere after.

Owned side

Marketing and membership

  • People who subscribed or paid
  • Monthly newsletter broadcast
  • Welcome flow for new members
  • Portal access and renewals
  • Sends from her own domain, on Gmail infrastructure

Separate side

Prospecting and cold outreach

  • People who have never heard from her
  • Corporate account targeting
  • Peer founder and podcast outreach
  • Runs in Apollo
  • Different list, different domain, different blast radius

No shared list  ·  No shared domain  ·  No shared reputation

06  /  Open on purpose

The one decision we are holding

Whether to buy a people layer at all

Supabase already holds the member records, so the honest question is what a CRM would own that Supabase does not. The answer is the human layer: notes on a person, and somewhere Shella can look someone up and see the last thing that happened. That is real, and it is not something Supabase gives her without more building. But at roughly ten members, it may also be a problem she does not have yet.

So it stays unbought, with the trigger written down instead of guessed at. Either of these two things happening is the signal to spend the fifteen dollars.

Trigger one

She starts forgetting follow ups, and the misses are costing real conversations.

Trigger two

She wants to pull up a contact from her phone between meetings and there is nowhere to do it.
