SisterGolf — Facebook Campaign

Drafted by ReRev Labs
For review with Shella Sylla

The case for a Facebook campaign built on one insight: golf isn't the product. Access is the product. Golf is just the room it happens in.

01

Where this came from

A Poker Power ad crossed my feed this week: "Learn life skills with poker," aimed at women in high-stakes fields. The formula is the thing worth stealing, not the words. Teach a transferable skill (reading people, staying composed under pressure, negotiating) through a recreational activity that doubles as the room where real decisions get made. SisterGolf already has that exact structure. Golf is the room. The skill being sold is belonging in it.

02

Who this is actually for

Not "women who want to learn golf."

Women in commission-driven, relationship-heavy sales roles who are already good at their jobs and are still watching deals close on a course they weren't invited to. Enterprise/technical sales, commercial real estate brokerage, auto sales, wealth management and insurance, medical device sales.

The actual pain

It's not "I don't know how to swing a club." It's "I get invited less, or I go and freeze because I don't know the etiquette, the pace, or how to carry a conversation for four hours without it going stiff or performative."

Positioning line to test

"You didn't lose the deal in the meeting. You lost it on a Saturday you weren't at."

03

What makes the messaging honest, not derivative

We're not copying Poker Power's words, image style, or brand. We're using the same underlying mechanic: a stigmatized skill gap, reframed as a teachable, ownable advantage, sold to women who are tired of being told to "lean in" with no actual instruction on how. That mechanic isn't proprietary. It's a positioning strategy, and a good one.
