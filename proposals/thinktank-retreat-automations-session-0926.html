Agent

The zero-cost system that turns your calendar and inbox into tracked jobs — works them while you're in a client meeting, and won't let anything go quiet.

The Problem

Running solo means nothing chases itself

  • 📥A client asks something on a call. You mean to follow up. Three days pass.
  • 🗓️A calendar block for "work on the proposal" comes and goes untouched.
  • ✉️Your inbox is where tasks go to get buried, not tracked.
  • 🔁The only follow-up system you have is your own memory — and it's already full.
What It Is

A worker that never forgets a job

Every commitment — a calendar block, a forwarded email — becomes a tracked row that gets worked, followed up on, and closed. You stay the one who decides; the system is the one who remembers.

Client follow-ups Proposal drafts Meeting action items Invoice chasing Content calendars
How It Works

One loop, running in the background

1

Trigger

A calendar event or a forwarded email starts a job.

2

Job Board

3

4

It reaches you

One short email — never a digest — with exactly what you need to decide.

5

It won't let go

Unanswered jobs get a short nudge every couple of days until you close them.

Under The Hood

Three pieces, zero monthly cost

Google Sheet

The Job Board. One row per job, one column per fact — status, decisions, what's done. No database, no app to maintain.

Apps Script

Free with Workspace. Watches your calendar and inbox, writes new rows, and is the only thing that ever actually sends an email.

Claude

Reads the brief, does the research, drafts the email — and stops the moment a real decision needs you. Never sends on its own.

Step 01 — Create Your Job Sheet

Give your jobs one home

1
Open Google Sheets, start a blank sheet, and name it "[Your Name] — Job Board."
2
Add one header row with five columns: job, status, next_action, due, notes.
3
Decide the three status values you'll use — queued, in progress, done — and stick to them.

Step 02 — Write A Standing Prompt

Teach Claude your sheet, once

1
Open a Claude conversation dedicated to this sheet — a Project if you have one, a saved chat if not.
2
Paste the standing prompt below once. It tells Claude how to read and update your sheet every time you come back.
3
Keep returning to that same thread — it's what makes the second visit faster than the first.
⏸

Pause here. Swap in your sheet's actual name before you paste — Claude needs the exact title to find it.

Standing Promptpaste into Claude

You're my job tracker. My Google Sheet is called "[Your Name] — Job Board". Every time I paste a task, add a row with status "queued." When I ask what's outstanding, read the sheet and tell me what's queued or in progress, oldest first. When I tell you something is done, update that row's status to "done" and don't ask me about it again.

Step 03 — Give Claude A Real Job

Feed it something you're actually behind on

1
Think of one thing you're genuinely behind on right now — a follow-up email, a draft, a call recap.
2
Paste it into the same Claude thread exactly as you'd tell a person.
3
Let Claude add the row and draft what it can — a first pass for you to edit, not a final send.

Step 04 — Set Your Follow-Up Rule

The one rule that makes it a system

1
Decide one number: how many days before an unanswered job gets raised again. Two is a reasonable default.
2
Add it to the standing prompt from Step 02, right below what's already there.
3
Now, whenever you open the thread and ask what's outstanding, nothing old gets to hide at the bottom of the list.
