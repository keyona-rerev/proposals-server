The moment she needed this

Rachel spent ten months building a relationship with a lead orthopedic surgeon at a large Atlanta hospital system, targeting a $1.8M implant contract renewal. She had the clinical case dialed in, the pricing, the follow-through. She was doing everything her playbook said to do.

She lost the account to a male competitor rep. She found out later, from a mutual contact, that the rep had played in the hospital foundation's annual charity golf classic with the same surgeon two months before the decision. Not as a formal pitch, just the four hours of course time that made him feel like a known quantity.

"I'd been invited to that same tournament. I didn't go because I didn't know what I was doing. That's the deal I lost."

What happens next: Rachel scrolls Facebook two weeks later and sees the SisterGolf "Everyone Has a Coach" ad. She taps it. Here's what she experiences.

Full funnel · back end

The stack running behind it

Ads → Assessment tool → Data & matching logic → Report delivery → Nurture engine → Booking

01 · Traffic
Ad platforms
Meta (Facebook/Instagram) as the primary channel — this is where "Everyone Has a Coach" lives. Google Search as a secondary layer for high-intent terms. Retargeting pixel on the tool itself for anyone who starts the assessment but doesn't finish.
02 · Frontend
Assessment tool
Same build pattern as this mockup — a single-page HTML/JS tool, hosted on Railway. No reloads between screens, mobile-first, feels instant. This is the actual live version of what you're clicking through above.
03 · Data & logic
Data capture & matching
Submissions and the tournament database (event, date, city, buyer-profile tags) live in Supabase. The "top 2 events" logic runs as a real query, so Shella can add new tournaments without a rebuild.
04 · Delivery
Report generation
A lightweight backend function assembles the personalized report the moment screen 05 submits, and sends it via Gmail API — so "90 seconds, ad click to report" ends with an actual email in her inbox, not just a screen.
05 · Follow-up
Nurture engine
Scheduled sends via Apps Script + Gmail. Before every send, it checks: has she replied, booked, or enrolled? If yes to any, the sequence stops — no double-touch. Full detail below.
06 · Booking
Booking & reporting
Consult calls book directly to Google Calendar. Funnel stage (started → completed → booked → enrolled) rolls up into a simple dashboard so you and Shella can see where people are dropping off.
Blocked on the Outlook/Google double-booking fix — can't send cold leads to a calendar that might double-book her
After the report

Nurture sequence

Up to 4 emails, never more. The sequence stops the instant any of three signals fires: she replies, she books the call, or she enrolls. Whichever happens first ends the sequence — no email goes out after.

Running alongside the ad funnel

B2B workshop outreach

Same idea as the tool, aimed at the companies that employ the women it's built for: get in front of sales orgs with a lot of women on staff, offer to host a training, and let the training itself be the on-ramp to enrollment.

Medical device & pharmaceutical sales
The seed example — physician and hospital-system relationships have run through golf for decades. Rachel's whole persona comes from this world.
Financial services & wealth management
Client acquisition is trust-and-access driven. Advisors default to golf as a relationship venue more than almost any other industry.
Commercial real estate
Leasing and brokerage relationships are built off-site constantly. Women brokers are a well-documented case of being left out of these rooms.
Enterprise SaaS & B2B tech sales
AEs managing large accounts where informal relationship-building — client dinners, golf, offsites — influences renewal and expansion.
Commercial insurance
Broker relationships with business owners and risk managers frequently happen outside the office, golf included.
Industrial & manufacturing distribution
Outside sales reps managing long sales cycles with plant managers and procurement — same relationship dynamic as medical device, different vertical.
Commercial banking & relationship management
Bankers cultivate business-owner relationships for years. Client golf outings are a standard, budgeted entertainment line.
Legal & professional services
Rainmaking partners and associates build client books where informal access — golf included — affects who lands and keeps the client.
