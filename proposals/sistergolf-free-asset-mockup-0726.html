The moment she needed this

Rachel spent ten months building a relationship with a lead orthopedic surgeon at a large Atlanta hospital system, targeting a $1.8M implant contract renewal. She had the clinical case dialed in, the pricing, the follow-through. She was doing everything her playbook said to do.

She lost the account to a male competitor rep. She found out later, from a mutual contact, that the rep had played in the hospital foundation's annual charity golf classic with the same surgeon two months before the decision. Not as a formal pitch, just the four hours of course time that made him feel like a known quantity.

"I'd been invited to that same tournament. I didn't go because I didn't know what I was doing. That's the deal I lost."

What happens next: Rachel scrolls Facebook two weeks later and sees the SisterGolf "Everyone Has a Coach" ad. She taps it. Here's what she experiences.

After the report

Nurture sequence

Two phases, one path to a booked call. Phase 1 runs the first week after she gets her report, on a fixed schedule. Phase 2 runs for up to 6 months after that, triggered by real tournaments happening in her metro, not a fixed calendar. Either phase ends instantly the moment she replies, books a call, or enrolls, whichever happens first.
Phase 1 · First week, fixed schedule
D0Same day
"[Event] is in [X] months. Here's your plan."
Delivers, doesn't sell. Recaps her #1 event by name and date, restates the prep-time window, and reads like a personal note rather than a marketing send. First and only ask: book the call.
CTA → Book the call
D1Day 1
"The $34,066 reason he's outselling you"
Reconnects the medical-sales gap stat from the report directly to her specific #1 event — what's actually at stake if she skips it. Reinforces urgency without pressure.
CTA → Book the call
D3Day 3
"He's not better at his job. He just golfs."
Same urgency as Day 1, reframed so the sequence doesn't repeat itself, keeps the "what he has that you don't" angle front and center.
CTA → Book the call
D7Day 7
"Beginners close deals too."
Handles the objection directly — beginner comfort level, ties back to the specific 4-month plan module that solves it (fundamentals → conversation flow), room for a short proof point once Shella has a client story to plug in.
CTA → Book the call or see coaching options
Phase 2 · Up to 6 months, event-triggered

Instead of guessing at a countdown, this phase waits for something real to happen. The day after any high-fit tournament runs in her metro, whether or not it was one of her original top 2, she gets an email. The pitch shifts from "get ready for what's coming" to "here's what you just missed," in real time.

+1dDay after event
"You missed [Tournament] yesterday. Here's what that probably cost you."
Triggers off any qualifying tournament in her metro completing, not a fixed day count. Real-time loss aversion instead of future prep framing, this is the emotional core of Phase 2.
CTA → Book the call
+1dDay after event
"[Tournament] happened without you. Your competitors were there."
Same trigger as above, rotates in as an alternate variant so repeat sends across the 6-month window don't feel identical.
CTA → Book the call
What Phase 2 actually needs to run: Shella's full tournament calendar for her launch metros, not just the top 2 surfaced in the report, since the system has to recognize every qualifying event as it happens across the 6-month window. Open decision: if a stretch goes by with no qualifying tournament, does the sequence stay silent (simpler, lower risk) or does it get a generic backstop touch after some threshold? Running alongside the ad funnel

B2B workshop outreach

Same idea as the tool, aimed at the companies that employ the women it's built for: get in front of sales orgs with a lot of women on staff, offer to host a training, and let the training itself be the on-ramp to enrollment.

Medical device & pharmaceutical sales
The seed example — physician and hospital-system relationships have run through golf for decades. Rachel's whole persona comes from this world.
Financial services & wealth management
Client acquisition is trust-and-access driven. Advisors default to golf as a relationship venue more than almost any other industry.
Commercial real estate
Leasing and brokerage relationships are built off-site constantly. Women brokers are a well-documented case of being left out of these rooms.
Enterprise SaaS & B2B tech sales
AEs managing large accounts where informal relationship-building — client dinners, golf, offsites — influences renewal and expansion.
Commercial insurance
Broker relationships with business owners and risk managers frequently happen outside the office, golf included.
Industrial & manufacturing distribution
Outside sales reps managing long sales cycles with plant managers and procurement — same relationship dynamic as medical device, different vertical.
Commercial banking & relationship management
Bankers cultivate business-owner relationships for years. Client golf outings are a standard, budgeted entertainment line.
Legal & professional services
Rainmaking partners and associates build client books where informal access — golf included — affects who lands and keeps the client.

Before any of this goes live

Technical implementation

What actually has to happen behind the scenes before this can run, not design decisions, access and setup.

Access
Facebook ad account
Need access to Shella's Facebook ad account to run the campaign. May need to set this up together, depending on how her login is configured.
Access
CRM or Google Apps Script
Need access to whatever CRM Shella's using, or we build this on Google Apps Script instead, which just needs to run on her machine or Google account. Done this setup before, not a big deal, just needs a call to walk through it together, probably an hour to an hour and a half.
Access
Claude account
Shella gets set up with a Claude account so she has access to the AI API this all runs on.
Tracking
Google Analytics 4 (GA4)
A GA4 property and tracking snippet installed on both the landing page and the tool, so traffic and conversion can actually be measured, not guessed at.
Tracking
Meta Pixel
A Meta (Facebook) Pixel installed on the landing page and the tool, feeding conversion data back into Ads Manager for retargeting and optimization.
